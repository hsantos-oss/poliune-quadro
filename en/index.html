Don’t ask if it can be done. Ask how.

The painting of Torres Vedras in our office was painted by foot. The artist, Dejana Nežić, was born without arms. This page tells who she is and why we wanted this painting where everyone can see it.

Video: Dejana Nežić, @girl_with_wings_art

Torres Vedras, roof by roof

We commissioned a view of Torres Vedras, our home town in Portugal, from Dejana. She mostly paints animals, so a whole town was, as she puts it, a real challenge, and one she took on gladly.

She worked on the canvas intensively for more than three weeks. She says she knew the painting would hang where many people would see it every day, so she gave every roof, every tree and every window the attention it deserved.

When she put the brush down, she says she felt proud: of the painting and of herself.

Dejana painted a town she does not know. Even so, you can feel the love and the passion in every brushstroke.

It was a challenge, and only one more in her life. We deeply value all the ones she must have met along the way, and how she has always found a way past them, with a smile.

Dejana Nežić, the girl with wings

In Serbia she is known as “Devojka sa krilima”, the girl with wings. She learned to do everything with her feet: dressing, eating, writing, painting and, later, driving.

She studied painting, was para-taekwondo world champion, is a mother and makes her living from her art. She says she does not paint disability or sadness: she paints movement, light and colour, in deliberately strong colours.

The Portuguese proverb has a second half that counts too: those who don’t want to, find an excuse.

We hung this painting to remind ourselves to look at the solution first and the problem second. Before saying “it can’t be done”, look for five ways of doing it. Almost always, one of them works.
